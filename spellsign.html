Spellsign privacy policy

Last updated: 28 October 2026.

Spellsign is a game for Android made by Gaijin Systems. This policy says what the game keeps, what it sends and where, and what Gaijin Systems receives (nothing). It is published at https://policies.gaijin.systems/spellsign.html, and the same text is in the app, under Options, About Spellsign, Privacy policy.

In short

  • Gaijin Systems collects nothing about you. Spellsign has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no crash reporting of its own, and it never contacts Gaijin Systems.
  • What the game keeps stays on your phone (and in your phone's backup, if you use one).
  • Nearby duels connect phones directly over Bluetooth. Your wizard's name, pronouns and moves go to the other phones in the duel; while you host one, the start of your wizard's name is visible to Bluetooth devices nearby.
  • The language-model opponent is off unless you turn it on. If you do, the game sends a computer wizard's view of the duel, and the rules, to the one service you choose: a server of your own, or a provider such as Anthropic or OpenAI, with your own key. That service handles it under its own terms, not Gaijin Systems.

What is kept on your phone

Spellsign keeps these in its own storage on your phone, where other apps can't read them:

  • Your preferences: your wizard's name and pronouns, the settings in Options, and how you last set up a duel.
  • Your record: your rating and title, wins and losses, your rivals, finest moments and hall of shame, and what the computer wizards have learnt of your habits. Options, Reset record forgets all of it.
  • A duel under way: how it was set up and every gesture and decision so far, so that it can be taken up again.
  • For nearby duels: a signing key, made on your phone and kept in the Android Keystore, which never leaves the phone (only its public half is sent; see below); and, during a nearby duel, what is needed to finish it or rejoin it: moves you have committed to but not yet revealed, and the duel's details, its host's name and yours.
  • A small file for the game's secret, which may hold a copy of some of your settings, and is left out of backups.
  • For the language-model opponent, if you set it up: which service it uses (the provider, or your server's address), the model's name and, if you give one, your API key or token for that service. It is kept encrypted, under a separate key held in the Android Keystore, and left out of backups.

If you copy or save a duel's chronicle (its narration), it goes to the clipboard, or to the file and place you choose.

If your phone's backup is turned on, Android may include Spellsign's preferences, record, saved duel and the details of a nearby duel under way in your backup to your Google account, and may move them to a new phone. That backup is between you and Google; Gaijin Systems can't see it. The small file for the game's secret, the signing key and the language-model key are not backed up.

Uninstalling Spellsign, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, deletes everything it keeps on the phone. A backup in your Google account is managed from your Google account.

Nearby duels

Nearby duels connect phones in the same room directly over Bluetooth Low Energy. No server, internet connection or Google Play service is involved, and nothing is sent to Gaijin Systems.

  • To find and connect to other phones, Spellsign asks for Android's Nearby devices permission. It is used only for nearby duels, and never to find out where you are. Spellsign does not ask for your location.
  • While you host a duel, your phone advertises it over Bluetooth, so that phones nearby can find it: the start of your wizard's name, how many wizards have joined, and what the game needs to connect. Any Bluetooth device nearby can see this while you are hosting.
  • During a duel, the phones in it receive your wizard's name and pronouns, the public half of your signing key, and your moves: your gestures and answers, sent first sealed and then revealed. The host's phone passes messages between the others. Each message is signed, so that nobody can forge another wizard's moves, but it is not encrypted; it holds nothing besides the game.
  • The other players' phones keep what they need of the duel while it lasts. Afterwards they may keep you in their record as a rival, under your wizard's name and the public half of your signing key, as yours keeps them. That is on their phones, and in their hands.

The language-model opponent

Spellsign can let a computer wizard be played by a language model, on a service you choose:

  • a server of your own, such as Ollama or LM Studio on your computer; or
  • a cloud provider, with an API key from your own account there: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Groq, Mistral, or another service that works the same way as OpenAI's.

This feature is off unless you turn it on in Options, and nothing is sent until you have turned it on and chosen a service.

When it is on, Spellsign sends to the service you chose, and to no other:

  • the duel as that computer wizard sees it: the wizards' health, the spells and monsters it knows of, the gestures it has seen, the questions it must answer, and the names the players chose;
  • the rules text the model needs to play;
  • your API key or token, if you gave one, which the service needs to know the request is yours;
  • when you test the connection in Options, a short test message.

It sends no account, email address or device identifier of yours, and nothing from your record of earlier duels. Like any server your phone connects to, the service sees your internet (IP) address. The feature is used only in duels on your own phone against computer wizards, never in a nearby duel, so other players' names and moves are never sent to it.

A cloud provider is a company separate from Gaijin Systems. What you send to it is handled under that provider's own terms and privacy policy, which you agreed to when you got your key: read them to see what it keeps, for how long, and whether it may use what you send. Its charges for using the model go to your account with it, and a long duel can make many requests. Gaijin Systems has no agreement with any of these providers and receives nothing from them or from you through this feature. On a server of your own, everything stays with you.

For this feature, Spellsign uses Android's internet permission and, to reach a server on your own network, its local network permission (on Android versions that have it), which it asks for only in Options, for a server on your own network. They are used for nothing else.

What Gaijin Systems receives

Nothing from the app. Spellsign has no servers, and it sends nothing to Gaijin Systems or to anyone working with it. It has no advertising, analytics, tracking, or third-party code that collects data.

Google Play is a separate matter. Google handles installing and buying the game under Google's own privacy policy, and, as for every app on Google Play, gives Gaijin Systems sales reports with limited details of each purchase, which we use only for accounts and tax. If you have allowed your phone to share usage and diagnostics with Google, Google may also show developers crash reports and performance statistics that Android gathers; these come from Android and Google Play, not from Spellsign.

Children

Gaijin Systems collects no personal data from anyone, children included. A child who plays nearby duels shares their wizard's name and moves with the phones in the duel, as above. The language-model opponent sends nothing unless it is turned on and given a service to use. It is meant to be set up by an adult: a cloud provider's key belongs to an account with that provider, which may charge for its use and has its own age rules.

Changes

If Spellsign starts to keep or send anything new, this policy will say so before the version of the app that does, both in the app and at the address above, with a new date at the top.
